How To Monitor DKIM Validation

    For Better Domain Reputation

    Successful email communication hinges on effective email deliverability and a positive domain reputation. Companies depend on email to disseminate newsletters, run marketing campaigns, issue invoices, reset passwords, and send crucial customer alerts. However, failures in authentication can lead to emails being directed to spam folders or outright rejected by the recipient's servers. DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM) mitigates these risks by enabling receiving servers to confirm that an email originates from an authorized domain and that its content remains intact.

    By routinely checking DKIM signatures, analyzing authentication reports, and addressing DNS issues, businesses can enhance email security and safeguard their domain reputation.


    What Is DKIM Validation?


    DKIM validation involves verifying an email's cryptographic signature by comparing it with a public key found in the sender's Domain Name System (DNS). When an email is sent, the sending server includes a DKIM-Signature header. The receiving server then accesses the appropriate public key to confirm the signature's authenticity.

    If the validation is successful, it shows that the signed elements of the email remain intact and that the signature aligns with the public key linked to the domain that issued the signature.





    Why Monitor DKIM Validation for Domain Reputation?


    Reliable DKIM authentication enhances email security and aids receiving platforms in assessing the authenticity of messages.

    • Enhanced delivery: Detecting failed signatures can minimize avoidable issues related to authentication and message delivery.

    • Defense against impersonation: DKIM plays a crucial role in recognizing messages that are unauthorized or not properly authenticated.

    • Timely error identification: Routine inspections can uncover expired keys, incorrect DNS configurations, and signing issues.

    • Improved DMARC adherence: DKIM alignment assists domains in fulfilling DMARC authentication guidelines.

    How to Monitor DKIM Validation Effectively


    1. Check Your DKIM DNS Records

    DKIM records are typically kept as TXT records associated with a hostname that is selector-based, like selector1._domainkey.example.com.

    To verify the presence and accuracy of the record, use a DNS lookup tool to ensure it returns the correct key. Look out for any typos, absent values, wrong hostnames, or incomplete entries. It's important to conduct these validations each time you switch email providers or modify your authentication settings.


    2. Analyze DKIM Authentication Results

    Examine the headers of emails to check if the receiving servers are effectively verifying your signatures. 

    • Pass: The signature verification was successful.

    • Fail: The signature failed validation, likely due to alterations in the signed content or a mismatched key.

    • None: There was no valid DKIM signature found.

    • Neutral or temporary: The receiving server was unable to confirm a clear result or faced a temporary issue with verification.

    3. Use a DKIM Checker Tool

    A dependable DKIM verification tool can streamline the process of diagnosing issues by testing selector records and uncovering DNS setup errors. Simply input your domain and selector to examine the published public key and ensure its availability.

    Several email authentication services offer dashboards that monitor authentication outcomes, pinpoint troublesome sending sources, and showcase any configuration modifications. 


    4. Monitor DKIM Through DMARC Reports

    DMARC aggregate reports offer essential insights into emails linked to your domain, detailing outcomes of DKIM validations, origins of sends, and alignment results.

    Make it a habit to examine these reports frequently to spot genuine services that might be failing DKIM authentication or sending emails without adequate verification. Look into any unfamiliar sources to confirm if they have permission to send messages on your behalf.





    5. Rotate Keys and Monitor Configuration Changes

    It’s important to safeguard DKIM private keys and update them in line with your organization’s security protocols and the advice from your service provider. Ensure that the new public key is published prior to modifying signing settings, and verify that outgoing emails are correctly validated.

    Keep an eye on changes to DNS, updates to selectors, and transitions between email platforms. Maintain a list of approved sending services to prevent missing any systems and creating authentication vulnerabilities.


    Best Practices for Maintaining DKIM Health


    Establish a reliable monitoring process to ensure minor configuration mistakes don’t escalate into persistent delivery issues.

    • Plan periodic checks for DKIM DNS settings and signatures.

    • Monitor authentication errors and explore any unexpected increases.

    • Protect private keys and limit access to signing platforms.

    • Confirm DKIM settings following any changes to your infrastructure or service providers.

    • Integrate DKIM oversight with SPF and DMARC assessments.

    • Examine domain reputation metrics, bounce rates, and reports of spam.
