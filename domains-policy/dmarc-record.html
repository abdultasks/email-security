DMARC Record Lookup:
Find And Analyze Your Domains Policy


Ensuring email security is crucial for safeguarding businesses against threats like phishing, email spoofing, and unauthorized domain usage. DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance) provides domain owners with the ability to dictate how recipient email servers should treat messages that do not pass authentication checks. Conducting a DMARC record lookup enables you to access your domain's specified DMARC policy, scrutinize its setup, and pinpoint any potential vulnerabilities in your email security. Regularly auditing your DMARC record allows you to confirm your domain’s settings are accurate, assess reporting options, and enhance email deliverability. Regardless of whether you operate a small business website or oversee a large corporation, grasping your DMARC policy is vital for bolstering your email security.


What Is a DMARC Record Lookup?


Looking up a DMARC record involves fetching and examining the DMARC configuration stored in your domain's DNS (Domain Name System). This configuration provides instructions to incoming mail servers on how to manage emails that do not pass DMARC validation.

Using a lookup tool allows you to check if a DMARC record is present, verify its proper syntax, and see the configured policy. Additionally, it can reveal reporting email addresses and settings for authentication alignment.




Why Is DMARC Record Lookup Important?

  • Determine Your Current DMARC Policy: Conducting a lookup allows you to see the policy governing emails that do not pass DMARC checks. Grasping this setting enables you to assess whether your domain is simply tracking failures or is actively safeguarding recipients.

  • Identify Configuration Issues: Errors such as missing records, invalid tags, duplicate DMARC entries, and incorrect reporting addresses can disrupt proper DMARC functioning. A lookup assists in pinpointing these problems so that administrators can resolve them.

  • Enhance Email Security: DMARC collaborates with SPF (Sender Policy Framework) and DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) to allow receiving servers to confirm whether messages are authenticated and aligned with the displayed From address.

  • Boost Email Deliverability: Authentication failures may hinder legitimate communications, especially when businesses utilize third-party email services. Examining DMARC along with SPF and DKIM enables administrators to identify obstacles that could hinder authorized emails from passing authentication checks.

How to Perform a DMARC Record Lookup


Verifying a DMARC record is simple if you have an online lookup service at your disposal.

  • Step 1: Input Your Domain: Access a reliable DMARC lookup service and type in your domain name, omitting the https:// part. The tool will access the relevant DNS record.

  • Step 2: Fetch the Published Record: The lookup will gather the TXT record linked to _dmarc.yourdomain.com. If it doesn’t find a record, double-check the domain spelling and DNS settings.

  • Step 3: Examine the DMARC Tags: Take a close look at the record to assess the policy, reporting mechanisms, and alignment configurations. Look for syntax mistakes and ensure the tags align with your security needs.

  • Step 4: Address Any Issues Found: Fix any incorrect syntax, verify reporting addresses, and make sure that authorized email senders successfully complete SPF or DKIM validation with the appropriate alignment. 

Understanding the Main DMARC Record Tags

Every element serves a distinct function.

  • v=DMARC1: Marks the record as a DMARC policy.

  • p=none: Asks for observation without enforcing any DMARC quarantine or rejection.

  • rua: Indicates the destination for aggregate DMARC reports.

Additional beneficial tags are pct, which indicates the portion of messages affected by the policy, and aspf and adkim, which dictate the alignment settings for SPF and DKIM.




DMARC Policy Options

  • None Policy: Observes authentication outcomes without initiating quarantine or rejection measures.  

  • Quarantine Policy: Flags problematic messages as suspicious, typically by sending them to the spam folder.  

  • Reject Policy: Asks receiving servers to deny messages that do not pass DMARC checks.

Typically, organizations start with monitoring to identify authentication issues, then delve into the failures, and eventually shift towards stricter enforcement once they verify that authorized senders are properly set up.


Best Practices for DMARC Record Analysis

To maximize the benefits of a DMARC record check, consider these guidelines:

  • After making changes to your DNS or email setup, always verify your record.

  • Analyze aggregate reports to spot any unauthorized senders and instances of authentication failure.

  • Ensure that SPF and DKIM align correctly for all genuine sending services.

  • Do not have more than one DMARC record for the same hostname.

  • Make sure that the reporting addresses are both valid and regularly monitored.
