How To Check DMARC Records

    And Identify Authentication Issues

    Email authentication is crucial for safeguarding business domains against spoofing, phishing, and unauthorized activities. Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC) enables domain owners to confirm message authenticity and instructs receiving servers on managing failed emails. However, an improperly configured or absent DMARC record can compromise security and disrupt message delivery. Understanding how to verify DMARC records and troubleshoot authentication problems is essential for enhancing domain security and email reliability. Visit www.dmarcreport.com for more details.


    What Is a DMARC Record?


    A DMARC record is a type of DNS TXT entry that instructs email servers on how to manage messages that do not pass DMARC authentication. It works in conjunction with SPF and DKIM to confirm if emails are permitted to use a particular domain.

    Typically, a DMARC record includes a policy, reporting options, and alignment settings. For instance:

    v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com

    In this example, v=DMARC1 indicates the protocol version, p=none advises receivers to monitor emails without enforcing quarantine or rejection, and rua indicates the destination for aggregate reports, which must be set up to receive them.


    How to Check DMARC Records


    Checking a DMARC record helps confirm that the domain has a valid policy and that its DNS configuration is correct. You can use an online lookup tool or perform a manual DNS query.




    Use an Online DMARC Lookup Tool

    A DMARC lookup tool enables swift examination of a domain’s published records. By inputting your domain into a reputable DMARC checker, you can access its TXT record. The results indicate record existence, configured policies, and syntax errors. Some tools highlight missing reporting addresses, invalid tags, and configuration issues. Regular DMARC checks aid in monitoring and troubleshooting post-DNS changes.


    Check the DNS TXT Record Manually

    You can verify the record with command-line tools. If you have nslookup, execute the following command:

    nslookup -type=TXT _dmarc.example.com

    Make sure to substitute example.com with your domain. This query retrieves the published TXT records for the DMARC hostname.

    On systems with dig, use:

    dig TXT _dmarc.example.com

    Check for a TXT record that starts with v=DMARC1. If none is found, ensure the hostname is correct, verify DNS provider settings, and confirm that recent updates have propagated.


    Common DMARC Authentication Issues


    Finding a DMARC record is only the first step. You must also identify configuration problems that can prevent legitimate emails from passing authentication.


    Missing or Invalid DMARC Records

    A domain missing a DMARC record does not have a clear DMARC policy. Additionally, records with incorrect version tags, syntax errors, or unsupported values may not function properly. Confirm that the record is published at _dmarc.yourdomain.com, starting with v=DMARC1. Ensure only one valid DMARC record exists to avoid conflicts.




    SPF and DKIM Alignment Failures

    DMARC mandates that a message must authenticate via either SPF or DKIM, ensuring alignment between the authenticated domain and the visible From address. However, SPF can succeed without proper DMARC alignment if the envelope-from domain differs, and DKIM can pass even if the signing domain misaligns with the From address. It's essential to check the configuration of your sending service to ensure SPF and DKIM are set up correctly. Resolve any alignment issues before implementing a stricter DMARC policy.


    Incorrect DMARC Policy Settings

    The p tag specifies how to handle messages that do not pass DMARC. The key options are none, quarantine, and reject. A none policy allows for monitoring, while quarantine flags messages as suspicious. A reject policy instructs receivers to discard messages that fail DMARC. 

    Transitioning directly to reject without verifying legitimate sources may inadvertently block valid emails. It’s essential to review authentication reports and address configuration issues before implementing a stricter policy.


    Best Practices for Maintaining DMARC Records


    To uphold consistent email authentication, it is essential to review your DMARC record whenever there are modifications to DNS configurations or email service providers. Maintain a list of services permitted to send emails on behalf of your domain, ensuring that each has the correct SPF or DKIM authentication in place.

    Begin by monitoring and analyzing reports, then progressively enhance your policy as genuine traffic is verified. Verify that the addresses designated for aggregate reporting are accurate and reachable. Refrain from altering DNS settings without assessing their potential effects on current email operations.
