How To Generate A DMARC Record Using A
DMARC Generator

Email security is a critical concern for businesses that rely on email communication with customers, employees, and partners. Cybercriminals frequently exploit email spoofing to impersonate trusted domains, launch phishing attacks, and distribute fraudulent messages. Implementing Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance (DMARC) helps domain owners protect their email identities and improve authentication practices.

A DMARC generator simplifies the process of creating a correctly formatted DMARC record without requiring extensive DNS knowledge. By entering a few configuration details, administrators can generate a record tailored to their domain's security requirements. However, generating the record is only the first step. Understanding its settings, publishing it correctly, and monitoring authentication reports are equally important for effective email protection.


What Is a DMARC Record Generator?


A DMARC generator is an online tool that creates a DMARC DNS TXT record based on user-provided settings. The record instructs receiving email servers how to handle messages that fail DMARC authentication and where to send reports about authentication results.

DMARC works alongside Sender Policy Framework (SPF) and DomainKeys Identified Mail (DKIM). SPF identifies authorized sending servers, while DKIM verifies a cryptographic signature associated with an email. DMARC checks whether the authenticated domain aligns with the domain visible in the email's From address.

Using a generator reduces formatting mistakes and helps administrators configure essential DMARC tags more efficiently.





How to Generate a DMARC Record Step by Step


Step 1: Choose a Reliable DMARC Generator

Start by selecting a reputable tool that allows you to configure DMARC policies and reporting options. Options include AutoSPF, MXToolbox, EasyDMARC, and other email authentication platforms.

Look for a generator that explains each available setting and produces a record compatible with standard DNS configuration. Some platforms also provide record validation and monitoring features, making ongoing management easier.


Step 2: Enter Your Domain Name

Provide the domain you want to protect, such as example.com. Make sure you enter the correct domain rather than a complete email address.

Before generating the record, verify that SPF and DKIM are configured for your legitimate email-sending services. These may include Microsoft 365, Google Workspace, marketing platforms, and transactional email providers.


Step 3: Select Your DMARC Policy

The policy determines how receiving servers should handle messages that fail DMARC evaluation. Most generators offer three primary options:

  • None (p=none): Collects information about authentication results without requesting quarantine or rejection.

  • Quarantine (p=quarantine): Requests that failing messages be treated as suspicious, often by placing them in spam.

  • Reject (p=reject): Requests that receiving servers reject messages that fail DMARC.

For a new implementation, starting with p=none is generally advisable. Review reports, identify legitimate sending sources, correct authentication problems, and gradually move toward a stricter policy when appropriate.


Step 4: Configure Reporting Options

DMARC generators commonly provide fields for aggregate reporting and, where supported, failure reporting.

The rua tag specifies an email address or reporting endpoint for aggregate reports. These reports summarize authentication results and identify sending sources associated with your domain.

For example, rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com directs aggregate reports to the specified mailbox, provided the receiving provider supports the configuration.

The optional ruf tag requests forensic or failure reports, although many receiving providers do not send them. Consider privacy implications before enabling detailed reporting.





Step 5: Generate and Review the Record

After selecting your settings, generate the record and review its syntax. A basic example is:

v=DMARC1; p=none; rua=mailto:dmarc-reports@example.com

This record identifies the DMARC version, applies a monitoring policy, and specifies an aggregate reporting destination. Replace the example domain with your own and confirm that the reporting address is valid.


How to Publish the Generated DMARC Record


Add the Record to Your DNS

Log in to your domain's DNS hosting provider and create a TXT record with the host or name _dmarc. Enter the generated DMARC string in the value field, following your provider's instructions.

Save the changes and allow time for DNS propagation. The record may become visible quickly, but caching can delay its availability.


Validate and Monitor Your Configuration

Use a DMARC lookup or record checker to confirm that the published record is accessible and correctly formatted. Then monitor aggregate reports to identify unauthorized senders, SPF or DKIM failures, and alignment problems.
