What Is A DMARC Lookup?
A Complete Guide To Checking DMARC Records


Ensuring the security of email communications is critical for safeguarding organizations from threats such as phishing, email spoofing, and domain impersonation. DMARC (Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting, and Conformance) provides domain owners with the ability to manage the response of receiving email servers to messages that do not pass authentication checks. By conducting a DMARC lookup, users can efficiently review a domain’s DMARC record, confirm its proper setup, and detect any potential vulnerabilities.


What Is a DMARC Lookup?


A DMARC lookup retrieves a domain's DMARC record from the Domain Name System (DNS), providing instructions for email servers on handling messages that do not pass DMARC authentication. DMARC complements SPF (Sender Policy Framework) and DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) to verify the legitimacy of emails linked to the sender's domain. Through a DMARC lookup, administrators can ascertain the existence of a record, evaluate its policy, and identify any configuration issues impacting email security and deliverability.





Why Is Checking DMARC Records Important?


Regularly checking DMARC records is essential for organizations to ensure effective email authentication and minimize the risk of domain impersonation by unauthorized senders.

Key benefits include:

  • Preventing email spoofing: DMARC enables receiving servers to detect messages that do not meet authentication and alignment standards.

  • Improving email deliverability: Proper configuration minimizes authentication issues that can affect the delivery of legitimate messages.

  • Monitoring unauthorized senders: DMARC reports offer insights into email sources linked to a domain.

  • Identifying configuration errors: A lookup can identify absent records, syntax errors, or flawed policy settings.

  • Supporting domain protection: Regular monitoring enhances an organization's resilience against phishing and business email compromise threats.

How Does a DMARC Lookup Work?


A DMARC lookup requests the DNS TXT record found at the specified DMARC location of a domain, usually formatted as _dmarc.example.com, where example.com is the domain in question.

For example, a DMARC record may look like this:

v=DMARC1; p=quarantine; rua=mailto:dmarc@example.com

This record contains three important elements:

  • v=DMARC1: Identifies the record as a DMARC policy.

  • p=quarantine: Requests that receiving servers treat messages failing DMARC authentication as suspicious, subject to the receiver's implementation.

  • rua=mailto@example.com: Specifies an address for receiving aggregate DMARC reports.

A lookup tool accesses the published record and assesses its format and settings. Nonetheless, a valid record does not ensure that all emails will successfully pass authentication.





How to Perform a DMARC Lookup


Follow these steps to check a domain's DMARC record.

  • Choose a DMARC Lookup Tool: Choose a trustworthy online DMARC lookup or DNS verification tool. Numerous utilities provide information regarding the published record, policy settings, syntax errors, and other essential configuration insights.

  • Enter Your Domain Name: Enter the domain you wish to examine, for instance, example.com. Most lookup tools will automatically retrieve the relevant _dmarc DNS hostname.

  • Retrieve the DMARC Record: Perform a lookup to obtain the TXT record associated with the domain. If the record is unavailable, confirm that the domain name is accurate and that the record has been successfully published at the appropriate DNS location.

  • Review the DMARC Policy: Review the policy value to gain insight into the domain's approach to managing authentication failures.

  • The three main policy options are:

    • p=none: Monitors authentication results without requesting quarantine or rejection.

    • p=quarantine: Requests that failing messages be treated as suspicious.

    • p=reject: Requests rejection of messages that fail DMARC evaluation.

Organizations ought to consistently evaluate outcomes and confirm the authenticity of sending sources prior to implementing more stringent enforcement measures.

  • Fix Errors and Recheck: Address any syntax errors, missing tags, inaccurate reporting addresses, or issues related to DNS publication. After implementing these changes, conduct another lookup to ensure that the updated record is accessible.

Common DMARC Lookup Problems


Several issues can prevent a DMARC record from working as expected.

  • Missing DMARC record: The domain lacks a record associated with the specified DNS hostname.

  • Invalid syntax: Validation failures may occur due to improper tag formatting or the use of unsupported values.

  • Multiple DMARC records: Establishing numerous DMARC records for a single domain may lead to an invalid configuration.

  • Incorrect reporting address: Aggregate reports may fail to be delivered if the reporting destination is either misconfigured or unsuitable for receiving them.
