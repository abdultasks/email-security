Office 365 Tenant To Tenant Migration
With The Same Domain: Risks And Solutions

Transitioning an Office 365 tenant to a different tenant while maintaining the same domain necessitates thorough preparation. It is essential for organizations to ensure uninterrupted email service, safeguard user data, and prevent authentication issues throughout the process. Recognizing possible challenges and devising effective strategies will enable companies to execute the migration smoothly and with limited interruption.


What Is Office 365 Tenant to Tenant Migration?

Tenant-to-tenant migration in Office 365 entails transferring resources between Microsoft 365 tenants, often necessitated by mergers, acquisitions, restructuring, or IT management changes. When both tenants require the same custom domain, complications arise, as a custom domain can only be associated with one tenant at a time. Consequently, the source tenant must release the domain prior to the destination tenant's verification and use.



1. Domain Verification and Ownership Conflicts

A significant challenge is transferring a custom domain between tenants, as Microsoft 365 restricts simultaneous active association of the same domain with multiple tenants.

Risk: Eliminating the domain prematurely can hinder email delivery, user logins, and services reliant on that domain.

Solution:

  • Verify domain ownership and review the DNS configuration before migration.

  • Prepare the destination tenant with users, licenses, and required settings.

  • Identify every account, group, alias, and application that uses the domain.

  • Schedule domain removal and verification during a controlled migration window.

Maintain the source tenant's operation until all required data and dependencies are resolved.


2. Email Downtime and Delivery Problems

Email disruption poses a significant issue during domain transfers. Users in the new tenant cannot fully utilize the custom domain until it has been successfully released and added.

Risk: If domain transfer and mail routing changes are not properly coordinated, incoming messages may experience delays, rejections, or incorrect deliveries.

Solution:

  • Inventory all mailboxes, shared mailboxes, distribution lists, and email aliases.

  • Prepare destination mailboxes before the domain cutover.

  • Plan the MX record update and confirm that the destination tenant can receive messages.

  • Test inbound and outbound email immediately after the transfer.

DNS propagation may take time, so keep monitoring email delivery throughout the transition.


3. Data Loss During Mailbox Migration

Transferring a domain does not include the migration of mailbox data. Emails, calendars, contacts, and user information require separate migration processes.

Risk: An incomplete migration may result in the loss of historical emails, calendar events, and crucial business documents from the original tenant.

Solution:

  • Audit mailbox sizes and identify critical user data.

  • Use a suitable migration tool or supported migration method.

  • Perform a test migration before moving all users.

  • Compare source and destination mailbox data after migration.

  • Retain the source tenant according to the organization's retention and compliance requirements.

A documented validation process helps confirm that important information remains accessible.


4. User Identity and Sign-In Issues

When accounts are recreated or migrated to a new tenant, users might face issues with authentication. Microsoft 365 identities, permissions, and authentication settings do not automatically transfer with the custom domain.

Risk: Users might be denied access to Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive, or other linked services.

Solution:

  • Create and license destination accounts in advance.

  • Map source accounts to their corresponding destination identities.

  • Review multifactor authentication, conditional access policies, and sign-in methods.

  • Communicate new sign-in instructions to employees.

Additionally, assess service accounts and application integrations reliant on current identities.


5. DNS, SPF, DKIM, and DMARC Configuration Errors

Email authentication settings are crucial for safeguarding domain reputation and preventing spoofing. While the custom domain may shift, its DNS records need not change unless the new configuration mandates it.

Risk: Faulty MX, SPF, DKIM, or DMARC records can lead to email delivery issues, authentication problems, or messages being marked as spam.

Solution:

  • Record the existing DNS configuration before migration.

  • Update MX records when the destination mail system is ready.

  • Review SPF to authorize the correct sending services without creating multiple SPF records or exceeding the ten-DNS-lookup limit.

  • Configure DKIM signing in the destination tenant and publish the required selector records.

Avoid changing unrelated DNS records without a clear reason.



6. Teams, SharePoint, and OneDrive Dependencies

Migrating a Microsoft 365 tenant involves more than just email; it also includes Teams chats, SharePoint sites, OneDrive files, permissions, and application integrations, each potentially needing distinct migration processes.

Risk: Users may encounter missing files, broken sharing links, unavailable Teams resources, or changed permissions.

Solution:

  • Inventory sites, files, Teams, and external sharing arrangements.

  • Identify which resources require migration or recreation.

  • Review access permissions and retention policies.

  • Test important collaboration workflows after migration.
